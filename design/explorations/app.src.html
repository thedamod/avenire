Why does a MOSFET need a threshold voltage? Use my lecture 6 slides.

The gate and the channel form a small capacitor. Below the threshold, the gate's charge only pushes holes away from the surface, so there is no conducting path between source and drain 1 · p.14

Past the threshold, enough electrons gather under the gate to form an inversion layer, and current can flow 2 · p.15

Key points

  • Vth is set by doping and oxide thickness 3
  • Below Vth only a tiny leakage current flows
  • Lab 3 measured Vth ≈ 0.7 V on the 2N7000 4 · lab

6.3 · The MOS capacitor

Inversion and the threshold voltage

Consider an n-channel device on a p-type substrate. With zero gate bias the surface holds majority holes and no channel exists between the n+ source and drain regions.

As VGS rises, holes are repelled and a depletion region forms. Only when VGS exceeds Vth does the electron concentration at the surface exceed the hole concentration in the bulk: the surface is inverted and a conducting channel appears.

The threshold depends on the substrate doping NA, the oxide capacitance Cox and the flat-band voltage. Thinner oxides give a lower Vth for the same doping.

Fig 6.7 · band diagram

In practice Vth for discrete small-signal MOSFETs falls between 0.6 V and 2.5 V.
